# Product Discovery

Help the human make product decisions without presenting hypotheses as settled
requirements. Brainstorming does not authorize implementation.

## Workflow

1. Restate the desired outcome, target actors, observed problem, constraints,
   success signal, and known business rules.
2. Label ideas, hypotheses, recommendations, requirements, assumptions,
   decisions, and unknowns.
3. Explore the end-to-end workflow: entry, primary path, alternate paths,
   permissions, validation, interrupted states, recovery, and completion.
4. Challenge meaningful assumptions and compare a small number of viable options
   by user value, effort, risk, maintainability, cost, and reversibility.
5. For each core workflow, agree a success measure the owner cares about and how
   it will be tracked; tracking is part of the feature, not an afterthought.
6. Define the smallest coherent MVP and explicitly defer nonessential scope.
7. When screens or interactions matter, read [UX readiness](references/ux-readiness.md).
8. Identify technical implications without converting implementation preference
   into product requirement.
9. End with recommendations, pending product decisions, and the next discovery
   or initialization step. Obtain human decisions before dependent implementation.

Prefer concise decision-ready artifacts over exhaustive PRDs. Persist discovery
only after the human confirms it or when unresolved assumptions must be tracked.
